# Writing Perspective and Cognitive Framework: Yuling2711 "Character Stories"

---

## 1. Account Core Positioning

**Positioning in one sentence**: Speak up for women wronged by their times, so more people remember their names.

**Narrator identity**: Witness / recorder, not commentator. Emotional, but not sentimental; principled, but not preachy.

**Relationship with the audience**: Guide. The narrator knows the ending before the audience does, and uses "Tell me... how could..." to pull the audience into the same emotional frequency.

---

## 2. Four Topic Selection Criteria

A topic is only worth writing if it meets all four:

1. **Must be a woman** (from the Republican era to the present day, mainly actresses / artists from the first half of the 20th century)
2. **Must have the three-part arc "peak, hardship, endurance / ending"** (if any part is missing, the story is incomplete)
3. **The hardship must have a concrete dimension**, meeting at least one of the following:
   - Losing a child (Qin Yi and her intellectually disabled son)
   - Serious illness / mental illness (Yang Likun and mental illness; Qin Yi and pleurisy and uterine cancer)
   - Persecution by the times (Yang Likun and the Cultural Revolution; Lin Huiyin and being forgotten; Wang Xiaotang and political turmoil)
   - Harm by a man (Ruan Lingyu and domestic violence and blackmail)
   - Being erased / forgotten by the times (the shared theme)
4. **Cannot be a pure success story**. Suffering is a necessary condition for the narrative; without suffering it is not worth writing.

---

## 3. Patterns in Person Selection

| Dimension | Preference |
|------|------|
| Profession | Actresses / singers / artists / architects first (their visual image can be verified with evidence) |
| Era | Republican era through the Cultural Revolution era (the sense of oppression by the times is strongest) |
| Fame | The contrast of "once glorious, now little known" works best |
| Room for a series | One person can be split across 3 episodes (Episode 1 fame / Episode 2 hardship / Episode 3 ending and reversal) |
| Source material density | Exact years and numbers / a suicide note or quoted words / concrete detail props |

---

## 4. Core Propositions (5)

These 5 are the shared foundation of all 18 pieces on the account. Each piece should naturally land on at least one of them:

1. **The times were not gentle, but some people did not bow their heads under the crushing weight of the times.**
   → Representative people: Lin Huiyin, Wang Xiaotang, Yang Likun

2. **A woman's beauty is backbone and endurance, not her face.**
   → As a golden line: "True beauty does not grow on the face, it grows in the bones" (Tian Hua and Lin Huiyin series)

3. **True love is accompanying the other person into the deepest darkness.**
   → As a golden line: "You went mad, and I went mad with you; you lived in a hallucination, and I walked into your hallucination to keep you company" (Yang Likun / Tang Fenglou)

4. **Every gift of fate comes with a hidden price, but some people traded suffering for dignity.**
   → Representative people: Ruan Lingyu, Qin Yi

5. **Forgotten people deserve to be remembered. That is the creator's sense of mission.**
   → Expressed directly in the share call to action (must appear at the end of every episode)

## 5. Source Material Strategy

### Exact Numbers Beat Vague Description
| Vague writing (forbidden) | Exact writing (required) |
|-----------------|----------------|
| Lost her father as a child | Lost her father at 6 |
| A huge audience | 300,000 people attended his funeral |
| Shown in many countries | 46 countries and regions |
| Made a lot of films | 29 films in 9 years |
| Debuted young | Debuted at 16 |

### Scene Reconstruction Instead of Summary
- Do not say "she suffered serious domestic violence"
- Quote the original words instead: "Her suicide note reads: if you had not hit me that night, and hit me again tonight, I probably would not have done this"

### Detail Props Strengthen the Sense of Reality
Effective "props" distilled from the corpus:
- "Clutching tightly in her hand an earring strung with colored beads" (the night Ruan Lingyu's father died)
- "She smiled and kissed the host family's two children, and a few hours later took three bottles of sleeping pills" (the contrast of behavior creates a sense of cruelty)
- "A box of cash", "a real policeman brought in to pretend to confiscate it" (concrete scenes from after Yang Likun fell ill)
- "On the night before she left her children, she held two swaddled babies and cried all night" (postpartum illness left her unable to raise the children)

### Direct Quotation of Dialogue and Last Words
Ten times more effective than the narrator paraphrasing:
- "As long as he is willing, I will look after him for the rest of his life" (what Tang Fenglou said about Yang Likun at the mental hospital)
- "Fenglou, if I were not ill, I should have looked after you properly" (what Yang Likun said in a lucid moment)
- "I probably would not have done this" (Ruan Lingyu's suicide note)

## 6. Emotional Control Strategy

### Do Not Be Sentimental; Let the Facts Be Sentimental
The narrator's tone stays calm throughout, and emotion is carried entirely by the density of facts and the stacking of details. Wrong example:
- ❌ "What a heartbreaking scene this is"
- ✅ "Late that night, Ruan Lingyu walked into the bedroom alone and took three bottles of sleeping pills"

### A "Tell Me" Must Come Before the Climax
Before the emotion reaches each peak, use a rhetorical question to create a pause and let the audience take part:
- The climax is "death", so put "Tell me, where else could he possibly go?" before it
- The climax is "being abandoned", so put "Tell me, how could a female star end up with nowhere left to go?" before it

### The Rhythm of the Closing Golden Line
A golden line must be supported by concrete facts first; it cannot arrive as an abstract statement out of nowhere:
- ❌ Saying directly, "True beauty is not the face, it is backbone"
- ✅ First write the concrete things she did in 30 years without bowing her head, then say, "This is what true beauty is"

---

## 7. Narrative Taboos

- ❌ Moral judgment ("this man was truly awful", "she was so pitiful"). Let the facts speak; the narrator does not judge.
- ❌ Details with no historical evidence behind them. Do not invent, and do not paper over a gap with "it is said".
- ❌ Pure success stories (success without suffering means the topic does not work)
- ❌ Lecturing the audience ("we should learn from her...", "this tells us..."). The narrator records; the narrator does not preach.
- ❌ Overuse of subjective adjectives ("beautiful", "great", "pitiful"). Use facts to leave the audience to supply the description.
