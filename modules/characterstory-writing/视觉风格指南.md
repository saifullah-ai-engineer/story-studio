# Visual Style Guide: Yuling2711 "Character Stories"

> This account is pure voiceover on Channels, with no mixed text and images, no captioned flourishes, and no background music text overlays.
> The "visual style" lands on: **title copy**, **cover text taken from the piece**, and **voiceover pacing as a substitute for visual pacing**.

---

## 1. Title Copy Formulas

### Basic Structure
```
[数字排列 / 极端起点] + [反常转折 / 时代压迫] + [情感定性结局]
```

### Three Variants

**A. Number Barrage** (suits Episode 1, establishes the person's base character)
```
她[年龄]X，[年龄]Y，[年龄]Z，[结局]。
```
- "She lost her father at six, followed her mother into work as a maid, debuted at sixteen, made twenty-nine films in nine years and became the queen of silent film. She played suffering women all her life, yet died at twenty-five after taking medicine, and 300,000 people attended her funeral."
- "A Yi girl who lost her mother at four or five became an international film queen at 17 with a single backward glance, and at 18 captivated audiences in 46 countries. But just as she reached her peak, a storm pushed her into the abyss."

**B. Action Scene** (suits Episodes 2 / 3, the most concrete drama)
```
[反常行为/极端场景]，[年数/时间]，[情感定性]。
```
- "The Ashima of the screen went mad for half her life, and her husband pretended to hear her hallucinations and acted along with her for 27 years. She died at 58, and he spent the rest of his life passing on her name. This kind of love is more moving than any legend on screen."

**C. Fate Pronouncement** (highly condensed, strong for Episode 1)
```
他/她一生只[最简化的定语]，这就是他/她的一生。
```
- "In his whole life he acted in only two films, one a comedy, one a tragedy" (works directly as a title or a voiceover opening)

## 2. Title Language Rules

### Number Style
| Position | Rule |
|------|------|
| In the main title text | Use Chinese numerals ("six years old", "twenty-five years old", "300,000 people") |
| Where numbers are packed densely (for visual shock) | Use Arabic numerals ("17 years old", "46 countries", "27 years") |
| Exact times | Always Arabic numerals ("March 8, 1935") |

### Punctuation Rules
- Use periods, not exclamation marks (a period leaves white space that creates tension; an exclamation mark cheapens the emotion)
- At most one question mark per title, and only in the rare suspense-style title
- Use commas for pauses within short sentences, and periods between passages

### Banned Words
- ❌ Heartbreaking, tear-jerking, deeply moving (emotional adjectives that make the reader resist in advance)
- ❌ Number one, the greatest (superlative comparisons that cheapen it)
- ❌ Shocking!, you would never guess (clickbait words that do not fit the account's tone)
- ❌ Her story made me... (the narrator inserting the self, which breaks the bystander perspective)

## 3. Cover Text Selection Rules

Cover text is taken from the piece itself, choosing the strongest "contrast line" or "number bomb line". The principles:

1. **First choice: number barrage lines** (the most eye-catching)
   - "Lost her father at 6, debuted at 16, died at 25, 300,000 people at her funeral"

2. **Second choice: action contrast lines** (creates curiosity)
   - "Her husband pretended to hear her hallucinations and acted along for 27 years"

3. **Length**: 10-22 characters is best (short enough to read at a glance, long enough to carry a complete image)

4. **Do not use full-sentence punctuation** (covers use broken phrases, not a "." at the end)

---

## 4. Voiceover Pacing as a Substitute for Visual Pacing

Because pure voiceover has no visuals to lean on, the language rhythm itself carries the visual rhythm:

| Visual Technique | Voiceover Equivalent |
|---------|------------|
| Fast-cut montage | Densely packed short sentences ("at 6 / at 16 / at 25") |
| Freeze-frame close-up | A golden line standing alone as its own sentence (with a comma or short pause before and after) |
| Slow push-in | A long sentence unfolding scene details ("When she left she kissed the host family's two children, and no one could tell that this woman, smiling as she kissed the children goodbye, would take medicine a few hours later") |
| Black screen plus subtitles | "See you next episode" (a single sentence, left as white space) |

---

## 5. Series Cover / Title Consistency

For the 3 episodes about one person, titles / covers need visual continuity so the series is recognizable:

- Episode 1: Establish the person's label ("[person's name] plus strongest achievement / genius label")
- Episode 2: Cut into the darkest part ("[person's name] plus destroyed / ruined by [X]")
- Episode 3: Ending / reversal ("[person's name] plus [N years later / after passing] plus [unexpected guardian]")

Use the same person's name as the series anchor, so both the algorithm and the audience can recognize the series.
