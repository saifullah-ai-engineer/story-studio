# Fact Verification Pack

The following are limited claims actually read in classical transcriptions on Wikisource, not a comprehensive fact certification of all 57 drafts. The web pages are public transcriptions, and important variant readings still need to be checked against collated editions. Classical original text and modern interpretation are used separately.

## F1 Han Xin: Changes of Post and the Bell Chamber Record

Source: [《史记》卷九十二·淮阴侯列传](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/史記/卷092). The full text was read this time.

| Key Original Text | What It Can Support | Expansions It Does Not Support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 至拜大將，乃韓信也，一軍皆驚。 | Han Xin's appointment as Grand General surprised the generals | Any inner monologue that everyone had long looked down on him |
| 漢王奪兩人軍，即令張耳備守趙地。拜韓信爲相國，收趙兵未發者擊齊。 | After taking the army at Xiuwu, he was still given the task of attacking Qi | That taking the army in wartime already proved Liu Bang had decided to kill Han Xin later |
| 漢方不利，寧能禁信之王乎？不如因而立，善遇之，使自爲守。 | Zhang Liang and Chen Ping advised Liu Bang, on the grounds of the situation, to accept Han Xin becoming king | That the kingship was decided only by affection or gratitude |
| 項羽已破，髙祖襲奪齊王軍。 | After defeating Xiang Yu, Gaozu took control of Han Xin's army as King of Qi | That Han Xin had already decided to rebel that night, or that Liu Bang already had a plan to kill him |
| 徙齊王信爲楚王，都下邳。 | Han Xin was moved from King of Qi to King of Chu | That titles of rank can be swapped freely without checking |
| 至雒陽，赦信罪，以爲淮陰侯。 | After being arrested at the Yunmeng meeting, he was pardoned on reaching Luoyang and demoted to Marquis of Huaiyin | That the loss of power was caused by a single sentence |
| 信乃謀與家臣夜詐詔赦諸官徒奴，欲發以襲呂后、太子。 | The biography does record a treason plot by Han Xin, and it must be stated | That every detail of the case has been confirmed by modern independent evidence |
| 呂后使武士縛信，斬之長樂鐘室。 | The biography's statement of the place and manner of execution | The bamboo pole, the palace women, and the "three things never to kill" promise |
| 髙祖已從豨軍來，至，見信死，且喜且憐之。 | The biography records Liu Bang's complicated reaction on returning and seeing Han Xin dead | That Liu Bang directed the execution in the Bell Chamber in person |

Note that "both pleased and pitying" (且喜且怜) describes the reaction after Han Xin's death, and must not be misplaced onto the earlier pardon. If only these materials are used, it is best to enter through the changes in military power, fief, and rank, without writing an omniscient psychological novel, and without deciding every dispute.

## F2 Jia Xu: Careful Association, Advice, and the Succession Question

Source: [《三国志》卷十·魏书·荀彧荀攸贾诩传](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/三國志/卷10). The Jia Xu portion and related Pei commentary were actually read; this does not claim every figure in the whole volume has been verified.

| Key Original Text | What It Can Support | Limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 詡自以非太祖舊臣，而策謀深長，懼見猜疑，闔門自守，退無私交，男女嫁娶，不結髙門。 | The biography connects his careful association with fear of suspicion; he did not marry into high families | It does not mean he never spoke in any situation, and still less that he offers a universal survival technique for everyone |
| 願將軍恢崇德度，躬素士之業，朝夕孜孜，不違子道。如此而已。 | When Cao Pi asked how to secure himself, Jia Xu advised him to cultivate virtue and keep to the way of a son | A modern-language paraphrase must not be dressed up as a verbatim original sentence |
| 思袁本初、劉景升父子也。 | When Cao Cao asked, Jia Xu answered with the sons of Yuan Shao and Liu Biao | One sentence cannot carry the whole causal explanation of the succession process |
| 太祖不從，軍遂無利。 | The biography states Cao Cao did not follow Jia Xu's advice before marching south | Pei's commentary evaluates that advice differently; do not write it as a strategist who never miscalculated |
| 此救命之計，何功之有！ | When Li Jue and others wanted to enfeoff him, Jia Xu called it a life-saving plan and declined the reward | These are words the biography attributes to the figure, and do not mean later readers must accept his moral self-defense |

Pei Songzhi's evaluation of advising Li Jue and the others to attack Chang'an in the west includes "how great is Xu's crime" (詡之罪也，一何大哉); if Jia Xu's merits and faults over a lifetime are written about, the public consequences of the advice must be included, and his whole strategy cannot be judged by his personal good ending alone. A trial draft that only discusses careful association and expression does not use this to claim he was faultless his whole life.
