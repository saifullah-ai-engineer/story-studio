# Opening Hooks: Categories and Multiple Versions of One Topic

## Basis of Analysis

All 57 voiceover body texts were read one by one, with manual annotation of the first sentence's task, tension cues, the first complete lead unit, how it advances, the body promise, and risks. The [per-piece analysis](../corpus/开头钩子逐篇分析.md) contains the original text and the first 20/40 character windows; the structured results are in `corpus/hook_analysis.json`, and the human judgments are in `corpus/hook_annotations.tsv`.

There are no audio timestamps, so a position in the text does not equal the actual first few seconds. Counting is by Unicode letters and digits, one character at a time, excluding punctuation and whitespace; 3 to 5 characters per second is only an estimation assumption. 20 characters is about 4 to 6.7 seconds, 40 characters is about 8 to 13.3 seconds, and the way numbers are read, pauses, and speaking speed all affect duration. An analysis window may cut a sentence in half, and cannot be used directly as a finished voiceover draft.

The median first complete transcribed sentence is 23 characters; the median manually delimited complete hook unit is 90 characters, about 18 to 30 seconds, so it cannot be uniformly called a "5-second opening". The tension cue's final character falls within the first 20 characters in 13 pieces, and within the first 40 characters in 38 pieces (cumulative, including the former). This is the position of cues under human coding, not the time a viewer stayed or a hook took effect.

## Nine Mechanisms

Each piece is assigned one main type based on the main source of attention in its first complete lead unit, totaling 57; secondary types may be multiple. The classification is based on how the information creates expectation, not on whether there is a question mark. K stands for an opening mechanism, and T1 to T6 are whole-piece structures; the two can be combined.

| Type | Pieces | Information Mechanism | Samples and Boundaries |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| K1 Fate and Treatment Contrast | 12 | Juxtaposes a person's earlier merit, power, treatment, or ending against the later state, leaving the cause of the change | H003, H018, H030; do not reason backward from the ending to assume murderous intent was there all along |
| K2 On-Scene Action and Dialogue Suspense | 13 | First gives a concrete action or one consequential line, leaving why it happened and what comes next | H007, H009, H044; in a historical piece, both the action and the quotation need a source; with no dialogue available, write only the action |
| K3 Revising a Familiar Judgment | 7 | Calls up an existing judgment, then offers a reassessment from a different observation | H005, H023, H043; the reversal needs evidence, and a guess such as a "secret deal" in the Empty City Stratagem cannot be packaged as truth with a question |
| K4 Condition and Behavior Contrast | 7 | A status, ability, or object value collides with abnormal behavior, cost, or effect | H016, H024, H039; unlike K1's change over time, the focus is on present conditions not matching expectations; folk stories must be labeled for what they are |
| K5 List Exclusion and Reveal | 5 | Sets a comparison standard, excludes familiar answers, or promises a set of differing facts | H006, H025, H028; not necessarily a ranking, and "number one in history" or "only ranked sixth" must state the subjective standard |
| K6 Present-Day Connection and Immersion | 4 | Enters the historical question from the viewer's choices, an object in front of them, or a present-day connection | H020, H029, H054; do not invent firsthand experience, and "without him there would be no today" cannot stand as a definite historical cause |
| K7 Knowledge Gap and Mechanism Questioning | 3 | A familiar object or system shows a phenomenon not yet explained, promising to take the mechanism apart | H026, H032, H041; first check the premise of the question, since Forbidden City rain drainage and sewage handling cannot be merged into one system |
| K8 Previous-Episode Continuation and Unfinished Information | 4 | Picks up a real previous episode or comment, and quickly gives new information for this episode | H001, H004, H008, H056; only suits an actual series, do not invent "last time we said", and do not let thanks crowd out new content |
| K9 Authority and Aphorism Opening | 2 | Borrows a locatable authoritative judgment or aphorism to raise a phenomenon needing explanation | H012, H036; few samples, check the original sentence, author, and context, since an aphorism is not proof of a historical fact |

Type boundaries allow discussion. For example, H003's first sentence is a funeral scene, but the core of its complete lead is a reversal of treatment, so it is mainly labeled K1 with K2 as secondary; the label cannot be used to claim the contrast was already present in the first second. H028 is a listing of contributions, and not every K5 uses a countdown to reveal names.

Common combinations: T1 can enter from a K1 change of treatment or a K2 scene; T2 can use a K4 abnormal choice or a K7 mechanism question; T3 often pairs with K5; T4 often pairs with K3 or K6; T5 often pairs with K7; T6 can use K2 or K4, and states the folk-story nature. Material comes first; do not force one topic to cover all nine types. K8 and K9 are not universal first choices.

## From the Opening to the Body

First let the listener know "who or what underwent what unusual change", then leave a gap worth questioning that the body can answer. Statements, juxtapositions, and actions all work; not every piece needs to ask "why".

1. **The first sentence gives concrete information.** A figure plus an abnormal action, a clear before-and-after contrast, or a clear dilemma of choice all work. "Do you know" or "what I dare not tell you most" adds no information. When background does not affect understanding, move the full dates, resume, and self-introduction later.
2. **Then deepen the same question.** Add a limitation, a cost, or a new fact, and do not restate the topic three times. A partial answer may be revealed early: H004 first says someone informed on him, and the questions of who and how it changed the situation can still follow; H005 first gives the judgment "endured", and the body still has to explain the concrete choices. Neither reveal the whole core mechanism at once, nor keep promising without advancing.
3. **Enter the path to delivery.** The next paragraph moves to the earliest necessary event, the figure's options, or the mechanism's steps, and does not start again from birth. The body carries the opening's promise with facts, and the ending answers the original question.

The contrast in H003 comes after background, H044 takes a very long first sentence to reach the arrest action, and H056 takes a long review of comments to reach the new question: these are compressible habits, not to be copied just because they passed 200 likes. A verified action may be moved earlier, but not by deleting conditions that change the factual meaning.

H016 and H046 open similarly on Diaochan, with 37,702 and 307 likes respectively at collection; H031 and H042 share a similar motif about how Han Xin died, including plot points pending verification. Likes cannot be used to rank hook types by effectiveness, and a repeated story cannot count as independent validation.

## When the User Explicitly Wants Several Versions

Trigger examples: "give me several openings for this topic" or "let me choose between different hooks". When only "emphasize the opening" is said, strengthen the one version chosen; enter multi-version mode when alternatives, comparison, choice, or an explicit number is requested. The number follows the user, defaulting to 3 when unspecified. A normal complete draft delivers one opening.

Settle the topic's core question, the verified fact pool, the answer the body can deliver, and the length requirement. Following the main workflow, read the five DNA documents in full and 5 related original texts, and combine this guide with the per-piece risks; when that is insufficient, fill in with the same genre. The `hooks` tool can search original examples by type, person, or era, but its results cannot replace fact verification. When there is no topic yet, run the topic-selection workflow first, and do not pretend the theme is already settled.

The question premise, numbers, quotations, and actions in the first sentence of every version go into the fact-check table per [Historical Facts and Sources](史实与素材.md). A question in historical mode cannot imply an unverified manner of death, conspiracy, or "what the historian concealed"; when the verified material cannot support a certain type, change the entry point, and do not keep an error because the hook is stronger.

Under the same fact pool, change the order of information, the narrative entry point, or the suspense mechanism. Cross-type versions on the same topic are allowed, as are different cuts of the same type when the user asks; do not only swap synonyms, and do not add secret talks, expressions, last words, or authoritative backing just to fill out the types.

Deliver each version separately with its type and entry point, an opening that can be read aloud directly, one sentence of body connection, and the delivery requirement. Types and explanations stay outside the voiceover; when only pure hooks are wanted, leave the explanations out. When no length is specified, usually write one or two sentences first, with the first sentence carrying the early attention task, and add an advancing sentence only when genuinely needed.

One version may be recommended, with an explanation of how it fits the topic and the length; do not predict a guaranteed hit. **When the user says to choose the hook first, stop at the alternatives, and write the body only after a selection.** When the user explicitly wants the full text at the same time, or authorizes choosing the best version, proceed directly; the existing word count and preferences stay in effect.

When "the first 5 seconds" is requested, first give a first sentence of about 15 to 20 characters that can be understood on its own, labeled with its actual character count and estimate; "the first 3 seconds" can be attempted from about 9 to 12 characters. This is an editing starting point, not a duration guarantee; the actual recording must be timed. Advancing sentences are listed separately afterward, and are not mixed into the "5 second" count. A specified exact character count is still strictly checked under the main workflow.

The selected opening counts toward the body's total character count; other candidates and explanations do not count. After a change, recheck the connection, delivery, factual scope, and total character count; when only the opening is changed, do not rewrite an already approved body without reason.

## Pre-Delivery Check

- Does the first sentence carry identifiable information, or does it first list a resume, praise itself, or dangle an empty tease?
- Do the multiple versions change the entry point or the order of information, and are they supported by the same fact pool?
- Can the most attention-grabbing claim be checked? The dying words of Wu Zetian in H052 and the bamboo pole and palace women plot in H031 are not to be carried over just because they appear in an original draft.
- Does the length refer to the first sentence, the complete opening, or a long lead? Do the estimate and the counting scope match?
- Does the body deliver on this version's promise, and was a full draft delivered early when the user had not yet chosen?
