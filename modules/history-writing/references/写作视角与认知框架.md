# Writing Perspective and Cognitive Framework

## Basis for Merging Across Accounts

Four accounts share a preference: open with an abnormal situation, use one person's choices to reflect power and relationships, explain historical conditions in spoken language, and then connect to real experience the listener can understand. Keep this shared method, while keeping the different narrative distances.

These describe only the corpus provided, not the full output of the accounts. The frequency of Cao Cao, Liu Bang, and Han Xin first reflects how dense the topics are, and should not be taken as words a new draft must use.

## Five Propositions That Are Usable but Need Verification

1. **Nominal power and actual resources can be misaligned.** H003 on the stripping of power and H041 on the inner court and the bureaucracy support asking the question this way. In writing, make it concrete: who can appoint officials, move troops, and control information, not just "the chessboard changed".
2. **Being useful and being trusted can move in different directions.** H031/H042 and H034/H055 use this repeatedly. A new draft should check the order in which actions happened; explain a figure's psychology only within bounds, and do not use the ending to prove murderous intent was there all along.
3. **An earlier decision rewrites the options that follow.** H007, H022, and H010 are not simply "do not be clever", but cases where behavior changed access, authorization, and the terms of exit. Find the intermediate mechanism, and admit that people cannot know all the consequences.
4. **Short-term cost and long-term judgment are not measured with the same scale.** H020, H028, H029, and H043. State who paid, who benefited, and how much time passed, separately; avoid using later territory or engineering to settle the bill for earlier losses.
5. **Replacing one person does not necessarily solve a recurring institutional problem.** H027, H032, and H041. Speak with the fiscal, appointment, or information rules of the specific period, and do not treat "dynasties fall every three hundred years" as a general rule.

## Topic Logic

The preference is for topics with a contrast of status, a clash between achievement and ending, a familiar judgment that can be re-examined, or a knowledge gap around an everyday object. Retrospection after the fact is common; with no reliable publication dates, there is no way to analyze whether publication chased festivals, hot topics, or timing. H057 mentioning Qixi only shows that festival customs are a topic, not that the account schedules by festival.

A working judgment for "a well-chosen topic": the question can be put in one sentence; concrete events support it; a limited length can deliver it; and it differs in substance from recently used topics. "Popular" here only means the samples the user provided with more than 200 likes, and cannot be used to estimate the success rate of publishing on any given dynasty. With only successful samples, no exposure denominator, and no low-performing samples, it is impossible to separate the effects of topic, account size, voiceover, and paid promotion.

This library has limited coverage of the Song dynasty, the present day, folklore, and objects. Do not infer that the authors reject other topics, and do not forbid a new topic just because the library lacks it; transfer a similar structure, and gather the facts afresh.

## Source Habits and Corrections

The samples often string together historical biography text, romance novels, hearsay, modern conduct analogies, concrete numbers, and book recommendations. The appearance of titles such as Records of the Grand Historian or Comprehensive Mirror in Aid of Governance does not mean a checkable chapter has been provided. Quoted text in the body must be analyzed separately from book-selling talk.

The new skill keeps the approach of "a concrete event carries the judgment", and adds source layering and timeline checks. Dying words, secret talks, expressions, and the historian's psychology in the original drafts cannot directly become evidence for a new draft. A modern analogy only helps the listener understand one local relationship; ancient ruler-minister relations cannot be equated wholesale with a modern family or workplace.

There is a lot of talk aimed at families, young people, and later life, so the default is plain, patient, and light on jargon; this is a reading need inferred from the language, not an audience age profile established by survey. Do not pose as an eighty-year-old elder, a teacher, or an eyewitness.

## Boundaries of Value Judgment

Do not attribute every good ending to "being good at calculating", or every death to "not knowing how to behave". Listing explanations is not the same as excusing the behavior; war, collective punishment, and killing do not become retribution just because the later regime fell. A historical figure can be judged sharply, but the judgment must rest on facts and their consequences, not on insults, forged monologues, or unproven motives used to add force.
