# Historical Facts and Sources

## From the Story Question to the Evidence

Original drafts are for studying narrative technique. What a new draft states as fact is decided by the materials actually checked. First list the key claims that support the argument and the plot, usually starting from 3 to 8 and adding more according to the actual amount of information, without treating the count as a verification ceiling. Every statement that can be checked externally should have a basis; ordinary background supported by the same source can be recorded together, while the claims that decide the opening, the turning point, and the conclusion are recorded one by one. A short draft can have fewer; do not pile up sources for form's sake.

Save the fact-check table in this project's directory. The minimum fields are: `ID｜Proposed claim｜Source and location｜Original text or faithful summary｜Source level｜Check status｜Scope of support and doubts｜Wording adopted in the draft`. For the source location, write the book title, volume and chapter, and the edition used; for a print book you may note the page, and for a web page note a traceable link and the date accessed. A summary must be labeled as a summary, not dressed up as a quotation. When reusing the fact pack, record the entry and the actual scope of support, without expanding it into a claim that the figure's whole life has been verified.

Use the statuses "source checked", "disagreement exists", "not verified", and "does not match the evidence checked". "Source checked" only means the way the material records it was verified, not that the historical scene has been proven exactly as it happened. Do not dress up a subjective confidence percentage as a verification result.

Give priority to verifying the names, dates, official posts, personal relationships, manner of death, scale of engineering, and direct quotations that decide the plot. For the institutions of a period, check specialist research; for artifact data, prefer official archaeological or museum material. An accessible transcription of a primary source is convenient for locating a passage, but it is not the same as a collated edition; important variant readings or disputes must be checked against editions. Many web pages repeating the same story do not count as multiple independent pieces of evidence.

Actually read the passage that supports the claim, together with the necessary context; do not rely only on search snippets, book titles, or someone else's quotation. First check the source's era, author, genre, and the scope of its account; even a primary historical source can carry a position, hearsay, and errors, and the label "official history" does not exempt it from scrutiny. For key disputes, extreme numbers, uniqueness claims, causes of death, and private dialogue, compare different sources or reliable collation research wherever possible; when no independent support can be found, narrow the claim, attribute it to its source, or drop it, and do not double-count a reprint from the same origin just to reach two sources. A disagreement between editions cannot be settled by counting sources alone.

## Factual Boundaries Before Writing

- **Time and identity:** List any necessary timeline as "time / the person's identity at the time / event / source". Check the order of enthronement, appointment, enfeoffment, death, and events, and do not use a later identity to explain authority held at the time. Distinguish nominal age from full years, and reign titles from Gregorian years; there is no year 0 across the BCE/CE boundary, and calendar conversion must not give precise months and days from impression. Keep uncertain dates at the precision the material allows.
- **Institutions and space:** The same official title may not carry the same power in different dynasties, the same surname does not mean the same clan, and a modern place name cannot replace an ancient territory without explanation. Check the administrative affiliation, routes, and whether the person could have been present at the time, and avoid having someone who had already died take part in later events.
- **Numbers and scope:** Distinguish what a historical text claims, what a modern estimate says, and what has been measured exactly; check units, denominators, and time ranges. "A million", "all", "the only one", and "never" carry an extra burden of proof, and must not be kept alive with a vague "allegedly" when there is no basis for the striking number.
- **Quotations and scenes:** A direct quotation must be checked for exact wording, speaker, addressee, and context. Paraphrase in modern language uses narrative wording and does not invent verbatim dialogue; a historical draft adds no unrecorded secret talks, expressions, actions, or dying thoughts. "Maybe" and "perhaps" cannot cover an unevidenced scene either.
- **Causation and judgment:** One thing happening after another does not automatically prove causation, later benefit does not automatically prove planning, and an outcome does not prove the figure knew the ending in advance. An explanation must give checkable actions, conditions, and consequences, and keep any counterevidence that would change the conclusion; do not summarize every case with "the meritorious must die" or "dynasties fall every three hundred years".

The premise of the opening and the topic question is itself a claim. "Why did Zhao Yun never enter the inner circle in his whole life" first requires defining and verifying "inner circle"; "who killed Han Xin with a bamboo pole" already assumes the bamboo pole is real. When the premise does not hold, change the question or give a sourced analysis, and do not use a question mark to slip past factual review. Multiple hook versions share the same verified fact pool, and any new premise in each version still needs checking.

## Levels of Wording

| Material | How the Draft Handles It |
| --- | --- |
| Clearly recorded in historical texts | Can be narrated on that basis; at highly disputed points, say "a certain book records", and do not treat the historical text's narrative as an error-free record of the scene |
| Commentary citing a variant account | State which kind of material the commentary cites, and keep the difference if it differs from the main text |
| Novels such as Romance of the Three Kingdoms | Say plainly that it is a novel plot, and check whether the original novel actually contains that passage |
| Folklore and folk legend | State the version or region, and do not treat it as a single unified historical fact |
| Newly composed historical stories | First label it literary dramatization; dialogue and scenes may be invented, but must not be passed off as a discovery from a historical text |
| The author's interpretation | Use qualifiers such as "judging from these actions" or "one interpretation is", and provide the factual links and possible counterexamples |
| No reliable basis | Delete it from the default historical draft or change the angle; only when the user explicitly asks for a draft pending verification should it be marked in place, without implying a secret history must exist |

Verbatim quotations in the body must have had their original text checked; a modern-language paraphrase must not be presented as a fake original sentence. Court secret talks, dying thoughts, and specific tears and expressions are especially easy to mistake for recorded fact. When the sources record nothing, say only that the materials checked here do not record it; do not infer that "the historian dared not write it".

When accounts disagree, state the differing records that bear on this question and their sources, or narrow to the part they jointly support; do not settle it into a single truth on your own authority. Plain voiceover does not need a source inserted in every sentence, but at disputed points it can naturally say "a certain book records" or "a commentary cites another account". A novel plot is not historical fact, and "romance" is not the same as arbitrary new composition: quoting a novel still requires checking the original, and newly added content is labeled literary dramatization separately. The user only saying "make it vivid" or "give it visuals" is not authorization to invent.

## Cases in This Library That Need Special Separation

- H031/H042: the Records of the Grand Historian, "Biography of the Marquis of Huaiyin", as checked here, records "beheaded in the Bell Chamber of Changle Palace" (斬之長樂鐘室), and does not record the bamboo pole, the palace women, or the "three things never to kill" claim; do not use this to assert that no later text contains the legend. The primary source does contain a treason narrative, so it cannot be selectively deleted and then used to claim Han Xin was absolutely never treasonous.
- H016/H046: Diaochan is a literary character; the three-condition negotiation at Cao's camp cannot be assumed to come from the original Romance of the Three Kingdoms. It can serve as a clearly labeled new composition, not as "the true ending the historian hid".
- H023: The modern reading of the Empty City Stratagem as a "tacit letting through" is not the same as proof from the official histories or the original novel that the two sides colluded.
- H040: Dying of illness cannot automatically lead to Cao Pi ordering a murder; the main text, Pei's commentary, and the author's speculation are labeled separately.
- H003/H015: The deaths of Lady Xu Pingjun and Huo Guang, and the fall of the Huo clan, are three dates to be laid out on a timeline first; do not mix year counts that start from different points.
- H026: Sewage removal, rainwater drainage, above-ground channels, and underground channels are separate; avoid denying underground pipes and then explaining drainage with a hidden channel.
- H049/H050: The same surname does not mean the same imperial branch; the fall of a dynasty does not mean the surname or the bloodline was wholly wiped out. H049's two accounts of Ziying's death contradict each other.
- H056: Check the time range of a cited book; the original Comprehensive Mirror in Aid of Governance cannot be borrowed to back the specific stories of the Southern Song Yue Fei or the Ming Yuan Chonghuan; find the sources that actually record these figures.
- H038: A contemporary figure's recent activities, quotations, and numbers must be checked against traceable reporting, and cannot be freely filled in the manner of an ancient legend.

Most of these are risk prompts that need checking, not a completed list of historical corrections. The small amount of original text already checked is listed separately in the [Fact Verification Pack](核验事实包.md). The `risk` of every piece must still be read.

## When Offline

Local reliable material actually read can be used, or work can stay within the claims listed in the verified fact pack. If the evidence for a topic is insufficient, first complete the part that has a basis, state exactly which material is missing, and offer a workable structure or alternative angle; only write a draft pending verification when the user explicitly asks for one, and mark the unverified parts. Do not dress up a book title remembered from memory, a guessed URL, or an unread search snippet as a verified source, and do not use "the sources are disputed" to cover up not having checked the material yet.

## Delivery Check

Check the final draft back against the fact table paragraph by paragraph, covering the title, the topic sentence, every opening version, and the body; after formatting, check again whether any new claim has appeared. The check order is:

1. Is every key assertion supported by a source actually read? Are the scope of the historical text, the chapter, the attribution of quotations, and the source level correct? A book title cannot stand in for evidence.
2. Are time, identity at the time, personal relationships, geography, and institutions mutually compatible? Were numbers and their limiting scope lost in shortening?
3. Was a single record treated as an undisputed conclusion, an author's judgment treated as a figure's psychology, or a literary plot treated as fact? Was counterevidence that would change the conclusion left out?
4. Does the body actually deliver what the opening promised, rather than exaggerating as it goes? Check the most attention-grabbing sentence and the most certain sentence; rewrite first where the basis is insufficient.
5. After word-count and spoken-language polishing, check the final changes again. Expansion may only add verified information; when the word count falls short, verify more material or adjust the structure, and do not fill the gap with newly invented scenes.

When there remains an unresolved key assertion without a basis, a misattributed quotation, or a timeline contradiction, do not label the draft as verified. First finish what can be corrected or deleted; state the remaining limits down to the specific claim. When the user only wants pure voiceover, the evidence table stays in the working directory, and the necessary qualifiers for disputes remain in the voiceover itself. The tool `validate` only checks file and corpus consistency, and has no ability to judge historical truth.
